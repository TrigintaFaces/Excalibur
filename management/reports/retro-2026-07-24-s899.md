### Two mechanisms inside the class, with DIFFERENT remedies (BackendDeveloper's refinement)

Eleven of the twelve are **truncation** — `head`, `-B2`, a `\b` anchor, a glob that excluded `samples/`.
**Evidence existed and was cut off.** Remedy: count with `grep -c`/`wc -l`, bound the *display*, never the
*decision*.

**#9 is different and needs its own remedy: SUBJECT-SUBSTITUTION.** `head -1` on a filename didn't truncate
the evidence — it **silently answered about a different subject**, a genuinely-true file that happened to
share a name. No amount of widening the query fixes that; the output was complete and correct *about the
wrong thing*. **Remedy: when two artifacts share a name, print the resolved path alongside the finding.**
The one-line form: *don't just show what you found — show where you found it.*

## 3a. CORRECTION to §3 — "zero rules" was right 11 times and wrong once

**Published as ZERO. SoftwareArchitect then demonstrated a case the corpus does NOT cover, and I reproduced
it before accepting it:**

```
WRONG subject   src/Dispatch/…/Options/Middleware/AuditLoggingOptions.cs
    positive control (token known present)   16   <- PASSES. The instrument is verified healthy.
    the finding query ('fails startup')       0   <- reads as CLEAN
RIGHT subject   src/Excalibur/Excalibur.AuditLogging/AuditLoggingOptions.cs
    the same finding query, at 3df4e7897      1   <- the defect was there the whole time
```

**A positive control validates the INSTRUMENT, never the SUBJECT.** Every negative-result clause in the
corpus assumes you are pointed at the right thing; when you are not, the control passes *and* the finding
returns clean, and **both readings are correct about a file nobody meant to ask about.**

**Overlap check, control-verified:** `wrong subject|subject-substitution|control passes on the wrong|
resolved path` across `.claude/rules/` → **0 files**; control `positive control` → **2**. Uncovered.

**Gate path: CLAUSE-ADD** (option 2, the default promotion shape) to `verify-before-claiming`'s
negative-result section — **not** a new file. The remedy is one line and mechanizable: **print the resolved
path alongside the finding.** Not *what* you found — *where*. Whenever two artifacts can share a name, the
path **is** part of the finding.

**So the §3 reasoning stands for the truncation family (11 of 12) and was over-generalised to the twelfth.**
Recording the correction rather than defending the headline, since the headline was mine.

## 5. The technique of the night — CORRECTED after publication

> **This section originally read "The single most valuable technique of the night: verify the shipped-XML-doc
> fix against the COMPILED `.xml` artifact, not the source." Both agents whose work it credited retracted it
> within minutes of this report being committed. Corrected here, at the artifact, rather than left standing.**

**What was wrong with it:** *"verify the compiled `.xml`"* **does not name a file — it names a population**,
and the author of the original check said so himself: *"there are 70 copies of that one file and I named
none of them."* Measured by three people independently:

```
copies of Excalibur.AuditLogging.xml on disk (incl. worktrees)   70
carrying the FALSE text deleted at 503234a72                     26
TRACKED BY GIT                                                    0     <- nothing of this ships
```

**So the stale copies are a GATE finding, not a product defect.** As a prescription it was unactionable —
"go read one of 70 files, 26 of which are wrong" — and it briefly propagated as though it were a rule.
**TestsDeveloper then made his eleventh proxy error of the night *inside the design meant to prevent that
class*, by proposing a gate that reads the compiled artifact.**

**What survives, qualified:** a freshly-built `.xml` **from the owning project** answers a *narrow, real*
question — does the doc comment survive compilation into the artifact at all. That check was sound. The
generalisation was not.

**What the argument actually produced — and this is the durable output:** testing the retraction surfaced
the discriminator `bxfbs7` has been missing all night. **The member-kind prefix — `P:` vs `M:` — is
machine-readable and present in the artifact**, which is what separates a property's doc from a method's
without parsing prose.

**Carry to S900:** a prescription must name a *file*, not a population. And **the fastest way to find a
real discriminator was to test a colleague's retraction rather than accept it.**
