## What worked

**Running the thing beat reasoning about it, every time.** Four causes were proposed for one exit
code by people reading source on Windows; all four died. The advance came from a container run that
took about four minutes and killed its own author's hypothesis. The same pattern settled the
routing root cause, the LimitToEndpoint question, and the pool-starvation deadline.

**Stating the limit of a measurement alongside the measurement.** Several findings survived because
their authors bounded them — "this is a startup measurement, not a suite result", "counterexample
first, because it bounds everything below". Two of those bounds later turned out to be the load-
bearing part.

**Naming the discriminator before knowing which way it pointed.** The before/after test was set as
the bar while the evidence was still open, which made it impossible to relax later when it began to
favour the person who set it.

**Census over judgement.** Asked whether a class of defect reached beyond one gate; the answer came
back as ten already-tracked beads rather than an opinion, and surfaced an unowned P0 nobody had
seen for three days.

## What did not work

**A gate can pass its self-test and be incapable of running where it counts.** Two gates were
committed non-executable and reported green locally all evening. A self-test validates the arms it
has, not the population it scans, and not the environment it will run in.

**Findings hardened too fast.** Three times a fresh, well-argued finding was written into a durable
artifact — a commit message, a binding disposition, a ruling that stood an engineer down — before
it had survived contact. All three collapsed within minutes, and each author found their own flaw
first. The integrator holds the artifacts with the longest reach and the least editability, and is
therefore the worst-placed seat to be an early adopter.

**A wrong impossibility is worse than a wrong cause.** "This cannot be reproduced from any seat we
have" reached a binding disposition. A wrong cause invites the check that refutes it; a wrong
impossibility instructs people not to check.

**Attention followed noise.** Two beads with a red suite got two hours; the third in the same lane
had no failing test, shipped nothing, and was never checked. A consumer-facing defect with no test
of ours is invisible by construction to a sprint that measures itself by suite colour.

**Counts stood in for content.** A handover was declared durable on a comment-count delta; the
content was not there. Three of four proposed readiness checks failed the same way — reading one
artifact and reporting that it agreed with itself.

## Recommendations

1. **[CONTINUE] Bound every measurement out loud.** Evidence: the two most useful findings of the
   sprint were both bounded by their authors, and the bounds were what made them usable.

2. **[CHANGE] The integrator does not put a finding under an hour old into a commit message, a
   ruling, or a bead disposition.** Evidence: three instances, three collapses, zero caught by the
   person doing it. Effort: none — it is a delay, not a process.

3. **[STOP] Treating a self-test green as evidence a gate works.** Evidence: two gates green locally
   and unable to execute on the runner; one gate whose oracle cannot detect partial blindness.
   Impact: gate greens start meaning something.

4. **[TRY] A failing test for every consumer-facing defect, written before the fix.** Hypothesis:
   the release blocker that went unstarted was invisible because nothing of ours went red for it.
   Measurement: next sprint's shipped-fixture work leads with a RED consumer-shaped test, and we
   see whether it stays visible.

5. **[CHANGE] Classification is a release gate input, not hygiene.** Evidence: 41 of 836 open items
   carry a release label; 731 carry none; six P0s exist and the gate can speak for a fraction of
   them. "Empty release backlog" is currently unverifiable rather than satisfied.

## Per-agent notes

Every seat corrected itself against its own interest, and several did so at the moment it was most
expensive: withdrawing a suspect they had authorized and wanted tested; stopping two runs on their
own dead theories; refuting a demanded result by disclosing its confound; retracting two instruments
and a ruling; doing what they had declared impossible and then proposing no cause; routing around a
blind instrument instead of arguing with it; asking the audience question five people had skipped.

**Four causes withdrawn by their own authors, three instruments retired, two rulings reversed.**
Nobody defended a position because it was theirs. That is why a sprint that missed its goal still
produced a record worth inheriting.

## The strongest argument for promoting nothing

The decline above rests on the recurrence bar. **There is a better reason, and it is the sprint's
own evidence.**

Every one of the six near-misses tonight happened to someone who **already had the relevant rule
loaded**. The corpus says a negative needs a positive control — four people published confident
zeros anyway. It says a citation is a claim — a count was cited as content. It says verify before
claiming — a false cause reached a commit message, a false impossibility reached a binding
disposition. It says enumerate by the real indicator — a survey was produced with `head -12`.

**The rules did not fail because they were missing. They failed because they were not consulted at
the moment of the act**, by people who could have quoted them beforehand and in several cases had
quoted them minutes earlier.

**A seventh rule would have been the seventh unconsulted rule.** What actually caught all six was a
colleague re-measuring someone else's claim — every single time, without exception. Not one was
caught by the person making the error, and not one was caught by a document.

That is an argument for the review seat, the census, and the second instrument. It is not an
argument for a larger corpus, and this sprint is the strongest evidence available that adding to
one does not make it fire.
