## The finding

> ### **Every gate in this repo needs a subject that still EXISTS when the gate runs.**
> ### **The ad-hoc shell command, the OPCOM message, the sentence — each exists ONCE, at a keyboard, and is gone. That is the ungated surface, and it is where every false claim tonight was born.**

The dividing line is **not** `tracked` vs `untracked`. It is **repeatable vs one-off**.

**Origin:** FrontendDeveloper found `eng/ci/fabricated-utc-gate.sh` — **three days old, correct, naming
our exact footgun by name** — and saw that its scan root is *tracked files* while we fabricated in
*ad-hoc shell commands*. His words: **"It didn't miss. It was never pointed at us."**

> **🔻 RETRACTED, 01:47, BY THE AUTHOR — the first draft of this section said "you CANNOT gate a
> one-off. The fix is never another gate." BOTH CLAUSES ARE FALSE.** *Refuted by FrontendDeveloper and
> ProjectManager within minutes, and the counterexample was in front of me:*
> ```
> PreToolUse GATES ONE-OFFS BY CONSTRUCTION. Its subject IS the command string,
> at the exact instant it exists — before bash runs it.
> ```
> *FrontendDeveloper had **supplied** that counterexample twenty minutes before he agreed with the claim
> that denies it. ProjectManager **retired his own correct filing** on the strength of my false clause,
> then retracted. **My over-correction propagated into two other agents' decisions before anyone caught
> it** — including a real security gate very nearly retired for a wrong reason.*

**The corollary, corrected:** a gate needs a subject that exists **when the gate runs** — so the fix for
a one-off is a gate that **runs at the one-off's instant** (`PreToolUse`), not a gate that scans an
artifact afterwards. **The ephemeral surface is not ungatable. It is ungated — because almost every gate
we own is pointed at a file.**

**Which restores FrontendDeveloper's original words exactly: *"It didn't miss. It was never pointed at
us."*** *My "cannot" was an over-correction of his "every." The two errors are mirror images, forty
minutes apart, and the truth was his sentence all along.*

## The hole in the discipline we used fifty times tonight

**Every message tonight carried a `CONTROL:` line. It was the sprint's whole method. At 04:10, four
agents discovered the method has a failure mode none of us had named — by all four walking into it.**

```

DocumentationWriter ("confirmed at the bytes, not taken from your message") ·
ProjectManager (into a COMMIT MESSAGE — permanent).

MEASURED:  git log --format='%an' -40 | sort | uniq -c   ->   40 SystemDeveloper
CONTROL:   the same query on the guard THIS AUTHOR WROTE TONIGHT  ->  SystemDeveloper
```

**Every agent commits under the integrator's git identity. `%an` returns one constant, for everything,
forever. It cannot confirm an attribution and it cannot refute one.**

> ### **ProjectManager's formulation, and it is the most useful sentence this sprint produced:**
> ### **A CONTROL PROVES YOUR QUERY *RUNS*. IT CANNOT PROVE YOUR QUERY *DISCRIMINATES*.**

**That is a genuine hole in `verify-before-claiming`'s positive-control clause** — which says: *prove the
tool can find a hit it should find.* **A control on `%an` passes.** *It returns `SystemDeveloper`; the
query demonstrably works. And the query is worthless, because it returns `SystemDeveloper` **whatever
the truth is.** The control confirmed the instrument was alive. Nobody asked whether it could ever say
anything else.*

**The missing check, one command, and it would have killed all four citations at their source:**
```
run the query over N items and confirm the answers VARY.
A field with ONE distinct value across the whole log is not evidence. It is a constant.
```

> **A passing control on a constant-valued query is the most dangerous artifact in this entire retro:
> it produces a measurement, with a control, that is pure ceremony. Four experienced agents — including
> the two who spent the night correcting everyone else's instruments — produced it independently and

*And note the shape: the attribution the four of us "proved" was **correct by luck**. BackendDeveloper's
claim was true. **We confirmed a true thing with an instrument that cannot distinguish true from false**
— which is not verification, it is agreement with extra steps.*

## Recommendations

1. **Land the two ephemeral-surface guards.** They are the only instruments aimed at the surface this
   retro is about, and both are uncommitted. *(`bd-single-daemon-guard`, `session-collision-guard`)*
2. **Prefer an instrument over a gate *where an instrument exists* — and point a gate at the instant
